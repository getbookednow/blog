How casting directors find talent in 2026 — and what most creatives don't know about it.

The process most creatives picture is outdated. Before a relationship gets activated, before a call gets made, a search happens. What comes back in that search determines who gets considered. Here's what that process actually looks like now.

The process most creatives picture is outdated

The version of talent discovery most creatives carry in their head: a casting director flips through a physical book, calls an agent they've worked with before, or spots someone at an industry event. Those things still happen. They're no longer the primary path.

What happens first — before any of that — is a search. The casting director, the brand, the producer has a brief. They open a tab. They type a description of what they need. What comes back in the next thirty seconds shapes who gets called.

If your name and work surfaces in that search, you're in consideration. If it doesn't, you're not — regardless of how strong the relationship with the agency is, regardless of how good the last job was.

What comes back in the first thirty seconds of a search shapes who gets called. The relationship still closes the deal. It no longer opens the door.

Where the search actually happens

It's not one place. That's the part that catches creatives off guard. A casting director in Cape Town might search Google. One in Milan might use an AI assistant. A brand's in-house producer might search Instagram directly, or use a platform like Casting Networks or a regional equivalent. An art director might ask a colleague, who searches LinkedIn.

The creative who gets found consistently is the one whose presence holds up across all of these surfaces — not just one.

That means: a Google result that returns something accurate and current. An Instagram profile that states clearly what you do and who you've worked with, in text, not just in images. A personal domain that works as the definitive reference point. A LinkedIn profile that a non-fashion professional can read and understand. Consistent naming across all of it.

What casting directors are actually looking for

Speed and specificity. They have a brief with a deadline. They're not doing research — they're verifying. By the time they land on your presence, the question isn't "is this person talented?" It's "does this person fit this brief, and can I confirm that quickly?"

What that requires from your digital presence: your discipline stated immediately. Your market stated immediately. Your most relevant credits visible without clicking. Contact information that works. A portfolio that loads fast and presents your best work first.

The 10-second test

Someone lands on your digital presence for the first time. In 10 seconds, can they answer: what does this person do, who have they worked with, and how do I contact them? If any of those answers require clicking or scrolling — you're losing bookings to someone whose presence answers all three immediately.

The gap that's costing you work right now

Most creative presences are built to impress — to show range, to demonstrate aesthetic sensibility, to project a feeling. That's not wrong. It's incomplete. The presence that gets found and acted on does both: it impresses and it informs. It gives the casting director the emotional confirmation they need and the factual information they require, fast.

The creatives losing work right now aren't losing it because their work isn't good enough. They're losing it because their presence doesn't pass the first-pass filter — the thirty-second search that happens before the phone ever gets picked up.
