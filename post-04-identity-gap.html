The industry sees you
differently than you think.
That gap is where
bookings are lost.

Every working creative has a version of themselves they're projecting — the work they want to be known for, the clients they're building toward, the level they know they're at. What the industry sees is often something else entirely. That distance is the problem nobody names.

This is the hardest one to raise with people — not because it's harsh, but because it's genuinely invisible from the inside. You're in your own career. You know what your best work looks like. You know who you've worked with and where you're going.

But the industry — casting directors, brands, agencies, potential collaborators — builds its picture of you from what's findable and readable about you online. Those two pictures are usually constructed from completely different information. And they're often very different.

Where the gap lives

It's rarely about work quality. The gap is almost always about how the work is presented in the layer where decisions actually get made.

A photographer with a strong commercial book whose portfolio leads with personal work — gets called for personal projects. A stylist with genuine TVC experience but one credit buried in an old caption — doesn't get called for TVCs. Not because they can't do the work. Because nothing about their presence signals that they do.

"Your digital presence often reflects who you were two years ago. That's who you're being considered for right now."

The market takes you at the value you present. If your online presence reflects an earlier version of your career — a past aesthetic, an older body of work, a discipline you've since grown beyond — that's the version being evaluated. The gap between where you are and where your presence says you are is costing you the right work.

The four things that create the gap

  1. Your best work isn't leading The work that signals the level and type of client you want more of needs to be the first thing anyone sees. Not the most recent work. Not the work you're most attached to. The work that earns the next brief you actually want.
  2. Your language is underselling you Read your bio as if you're a stranger. Does it describe someone worth hiring at the rate you want to charge? Does it communicate authority and a specific point of view — or does it hedge, stay vague, and avoid claiming too much?
  3. Your credits aren't visible If a brand or publication you've worked with isn't explicitly named somewhere findable, it doesn't exist in the industry's picture of you. Some discretion is warranted. But if you're being modest about work that would change how you're perceived, that modesty is working against you.
  4. Your positioning is too broad Available for everything means right for nothing in particular. Creatives who get called consistently for the work they want are specific about what they do. Not because they've closed doors — because they've made it easy for the right client to recognise them immediately.
What closing the gap looks like

It's not reinvention. It's alignment. Making the gap between where you are and where your presence says you are as small as possible — so that the industry's picture of you matches the one you already have of yourself. That's the whole job. That's what Signal works on.

A useful exercise

Search your own name. Spend two minutes on what comes up, as if you're a stranger with a brief to fill. What level do you land at? What discipline comes through? What type of work would you call this person for?

If the answer doesn't match where you actually are — that's the gap. It's fixable. But it requires looking at it directly rather than assuming it's fine because you know the truth of it from the inside.
