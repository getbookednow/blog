The five things a casting director checks before they call you.

Before a casting director picks up the phone, they've already verified five things. Most creatives don't know what those five things are. Most creatives are failing at least two of them.

Not in a specific order. Not always consciously. But consistently — across markets, across disciplines, across the different ways search happens now. These are the five things that determine whether the call gets made.

The creative who gets called isn't always the most talented in the room. They're the one who was easiest to verify.
  1. Your name is findable and consistent. This sounds obvious. It isn't done. Search your own name right now — not logged in, incognito mode, as a stranger would. What comes back? Is it you? Is it current? Is the same name used the same way across every platform? Inconsistency signals unreliability to search systems. Casting directors search names. If the results are fragmented, contradictory, or simply empty — the search ends there.
  2. Your discipline is stated in plain text. Images do not tell a casting director what you do. A portfolio of stunning images with no written description of your role, your specialisation, or your market is a portfolio they cannot search, cannot quote to a client, and cannot quickly confirm matches their brief. Your discipline needs to exist in words — on your site, in your bio, in your agency profile. "Fashion photographer specialising in editorial and campaign work" is more findable than a hundred images and no caption.
  3. Your credits are visible without asking. The question a casting director is answering when they look at your presence is: has this person done work at the level this brief requires? If your credits aren't on your site, aren't in your bio, aren't findable in a search — they can't answer that question without calling someone to ask. Most don't call. They move on to the next name where the answer is already there.
  4. You have an owned presence outside social media. Instagram is not a professional portfolio. It's a discovery tool. A casting director who finds you on Instagram is looking for a link out — to a site, to a proper portfolio, to something that gives them the full picture. If that link doesn't exist, or leads to something incomplete, the impression your Instagram made doesn't convert into a booking. The owned presence is what closes the gap between being found and being hired.
  5. Contact is immediate and unambiguous. This one is lost more often than any other. A casting director is on a brief with a deadline. They find you. They want to reach out. There is no email address visible. The contact form hasn't worked since the site was built. The DM inbox is on a profile set to message requests. The WhatsApp number in the bio goes to an old phone. Every extra step between finding you and contacting you is a booking that doesn't happen. One clear, working contact point — visible without scrolling — is not optional.

The pattern

These five things aren't talent markers. They're infrastructure markers. They exist independently of how good the work is. And they compound: a creative who scores well on all five gets found more, gets called more, gets booked more — which generates more credits, which strengthens the presence further.

The creative who scores poorly on even two of these five consistently loses the first-pass filter. Not because they weren't good enough. Because they weren't easy enough to verify.
